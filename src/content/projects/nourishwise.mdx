## The problem

Most nutrition apps are built for losing weight. After birth, a mother's body
needs something different: recovery from the delivery, often breastfeeding, and
almost no time or energy left to plan meals. A generic calorie target doesn't
fit, and the first months can feel lonely.

NourishWise is a daily companion for that stretch: targets worked out for her,
meals planned for her, and other mothers to talk to. For the app, that meant
targets that depend on how she delivered, her baby's age and how she feeds;
daily screens that take seconds to use; and a community that feels instant on a
phone.

## My role

I was the only mobile engineer. I built the whole app, from sign-in to
subscriptions, and shipped it to the App Store and Google Play. I worked with
our backend team on the API it runs on.

## How it works

### What people use

- **Onboarding** asks about the delivery, the baby's age and feeding, then works
  out personal calorie and macro targets.
- **A daily dashboard** with mood check-ins, the day's meals, recommendations
  and a nutrition graph, plus a notification when a new meal plan is ready.
- **Recipes** with nutrition per serving, favourites, and recipes users write
  themselves.
- **Groceries** straight from recipes, with duplicates merged.
- **Progress:** nutrition and mood trends, streaks, and badges that can be
  shared with the community.
- **Community forums** with posts, comments, likes and follows.
- **Premium** features through a subscription.

### Under the hood

An Expo and React Native app. Sign-in runs through Supabase in four ways: an
email code, Google, Apple and Facebook. Everything else goes through a typed
service layer to our REST API, with React Query for server data and Zustand for
app state. Subscriptions run on RevenueCat, and fixes ship over the air through
EAS update channels.

## Decisions

**Browse free, pay per feature.** The first version put the paywall at the door.
I replaced it with gates on individual premium actions, so people can see the
value before they're asked to pay. The cost: every premium action needs its own
gate and its own upsell.

**The maths lives on the server.** Calorie targets, recipe nutrition and the
analytics are all computed by the API, so the app stays thin and every screen
agrees. When someone edits a recipe's ingredients, its nutrition is marked stale
and worked out again. The cost: some of that work runs through AI and takes
time, so the app shows a "meals preparing" state and waits longer than a normal
request would.

**Updates over the air.** Most fixes reach phones without waiting for store
review. The cost: anything that touches native code still needs a full store
release.

## The hard part

**One subscription, two stores.** Premium has to behave the same on an iPhone
and an Android phone, for people who signed up in four different ways. Under the
hood, the App Store and Google Play model subscriptions differently, and every
difference showed up as a confused customer.

- **Every sign-in has to lead to the same customer.** At first, people who
  signed up with an email code weren't linked to their subscription account, so
  their premium status couldn't follow them. Now every sign-in method ends in
  the same link.
- **Android thinks in plans.** On Google Play, monthly and yearly are two plans
  of one subscription, and RevenueCat's ready-made "manage subscription" screen
  couldn't switch between them there. I built a custom flow for Android: the
  plan you're not on is pre-selected, and cancelling takes you straight to Play.
- **One source of truth.** Subscription logic had grown across screens. I moved
  it into one place with five clear states: free, trial, active, cancelled and
  expired.

What it taught me: "one subscription" is really two different store systems
wearing the same name. Design for both from day one.

**Another one, briefly.** In the community feed, likes and comments appear the
moment you tap, before the server answers. If the server refuses, the change
rolls back cleanly, and it has to stay consistent across the feed, the post page
and the profile at the same time.

## Outcome so far

NourishWise has been live on the App Store and Google Play since October 2025,
and is now at version 1.0.2.

What I'd change: I'd write tests from the start, because it shipped with almost
none. And I'd build the Android subscription flow before the iOS one, since
Android's rules shaped everything in the end.
